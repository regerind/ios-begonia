## The boxes

`eagl.c` kept the textures that are IOSurfaces in a table of 256. With the table full, `-texImageIOSurface` gave up
("more textures with IOSurfaces than there is room for": 298 times in x22, 68 in x48, 112 in x49, never on the home
screen alone) and the texture stayed with no picture at all. CoreAnimation drew with it all the same, and a texture
without storage samples as opaque black. x48's screenshot shows whole columns of the emoji grid black after a fast
scroll; x49's shows single cells still black 14 s later, and its check of every bound texture found 438 with no
storage, all CoreAnimation's. Userland 0025 makes the table 2048 with a lookup hint: x50 has no refusal, no such
texture in 15,016 looked at, and a whole emoji grid in the screenshot.

Everything tried before this (R8 swizzles, one mip level, sharing masks, 64-byte rows) was about textures that did
have a picture. Texture state was never wrong (x30 on: 3,000 looks a session).

## What made the speed, in order of effect

1. **The DRM shim's lock and the per-frame fence (x44: Mesa 0005 + no fence).** 60% to 87% of intervals under 18 ms,
   49 to 21 us a draw. The begonia sync shim (`src/hakios-drm/hakios-native-sync.c`) took a global mutex on every
   `drmIoctl` and broadcast a condition at every submit, against a helper thread; the bridge asked for a fence every frame,
   which made a pipe and a sync file each time. Mesa 0004 and 0005 stop the lock and the broadcast; the fence is off by default.
2. **Mesa unmapping every buffer it put in its cache (x37: Mesa 0003).** Upstream panfrost unmaps a buffer when its last
   reference goes and maps it again on reuse; here that is a Mach message, a `mach_vm_deallocate` and page faults.
   2646 map requests in 5 s became 19 to 86; frame time 17.2 to 14.4 ms.
3. **Copying a new, empty display surface into the frame's target (x45: userland 0022).** CoreAnimation makes a new
   display surface for nearly every frame and the bridge had the GPU copy its zeroes: a full-screen job a frame. It is
   cleared instead. 87% to 98%.
4. **Cleaning the CPU cache for whole surfaces at every submit (`mcs=1`, now the default).** Halved the submit request.
5. **Buffer looks answered in the shim (Mesa 0004)**, **no madvise round trips (Mesa 0002)**, **wrapped surfaces not
   hashed (userland 0015)**: smaller, measured gains each.

Tried and not kept: the GPU job thread on an A55 (x34: no change, kept as it is harmless); drawing straight into the
surface (`glblit=0`, x47: fragment jobs 600 to 700 us, more late frames); relaxing panfrost's 64-byte row rule (not
tried: research says it is a hardware limit on this GPU generation, an 8-bit image with 480-byte rows faulted on a G52);
running tiler and fragment jobs side by side (not possible: Mesa gives all batches one tiler heap).

## Tearing

The display driver already knew how to write each share of the rows behind the panel's scan (`fb_beam`); it lacked a
line counter for this SoC. Kernel 0013 gives it DISP_RDMA0's (`fb_line=0x1400b12c`), which is now the default, 20 s
after the driver starts (`fb_beam_wait=`). The driver counts shares written across the scan: none in x46, x48, x49.
With it on, intervals spread a little (x46: 76% under 18 ms, 23% between 18 and 26, none over 35): every frame still
has a refresh of its own. `fb_line=0` puts frames back on their beat.

## How it was found

- **Per-call timing in the bridge** (userland 0017, 0018, 0021): time inside GL calls against CoreAnimation's own, by
  call, and Mesa's own draw against the bridge around it. CoreAnimation was never the cost (1 to 2 ms a frame).
- **Flat sampling profile** (userland 0020, `glprof=2000`): by 64-byte piece of each image and by image. The thirty
  stacks printed before were a third of the samples. It showed 41% of the drawing thread in kernel calls, and
  `__psynch_mutexwait` (first misread as `mach_vm_deallocate`: `dladdr` names the nearest exported symbol).
  `C:\begonia-private\sh\sym.sh`, `prof.py` and `flat.py` symbolize against the unstripped libOSMesa.
- **Kernel counters** (`mcp=1`): cache cleaning by stage and every GPU request by kind, count and time
  (`mali-cache:`, `mali-calls`), GPU jobs by kind and by length (`BEGONIA GPU JOBS`, `BEGONIA GPU SIZES`).
- **The frame-interval histogram** of the display driver ("from one frame to the next") is the measure to use.
  Frames a second over five seconds is not: it counts the pauses between gestures.
- **Screenshots in the log** (kernel 0015): the boxes were seen, counted and seen gone without anybody at the phone.
