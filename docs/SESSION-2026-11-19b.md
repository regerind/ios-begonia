## The boxes

`eagl.c` kept the textures that are IOSurfaces in a table of 256. With the table full, `-texImageIOSurface` gave up
("more textures with IOSurfaces than there is room for": 298 times in x22, 68 in x48, 112 in x49, never on the home
screen alone) and the texture stayed with no picture at all. CoreAnimation drew with it all the same, and a texture
without storage samples as opaque black. x48's screenshot shows whole columns of the emoji grid black after a fast
scroll; x49's shows single cells still black 14 s later, and its check of every bound texture found 438 with no
storage, all CoreAnimation's. Userland 0025 makes the table 2048 with a lookup hint: x50 has no refusal, no such
texture in 15,016 looked at, and a whole emoji grid in the screenshot.

Everything tried before this (R8 swizzles, one mip level, sharing masks, 64-byte rows) was about textures that did
have a picture. Texture state was never wrong (x30 on: 3,000 looks a session).

## What made the speed, in order of effect

1. **The DRM shim's lock and the per-frame fence (x44: Mesa 0005 + no fence).** 60% to 87% of intervals under 18 ms,
   49 to 21 us a draw. The begonia sync shim (`src/hakios-drm/hakios-native-sync.c`) took a global mutex on every
   `drmIoctl` and broadcast a condition at every submit, against a helper thread; the bridge asked for a fence every frame,
   which made a pipe and a sync file each time. Mesa 0004 and 0005 stop the lock and the broadcast; the fence is off by default.
2. **Mesa unmapping every buffer it put in its cache (x37: Mesa 0003).** Upstream panfrost unmaps a buffer when its last
   reference goes and maps it again on reuse; here that is a Mach message, a `mach_vm_deallocate` and page faults.
   2646 map requests in 5 s became 19 to 86; frame time 17.2 to 14.4 ms.
3. **Copying a new, empty display surface into the frame's target (x45: userland 0022).** CoreAnimation makes a new
   display surface for nearly every frame and the bridge had the GPU copy its zeroes: a full-screen job a frame. It is
   cleared instead. 87% to 98%.
4. **Cleaning the CPU cache for whole surfaces at every submit (`mcs=1`, now the default).** Halved the submit request.
5. **Buffer looks answered in the shim (Mesa 0004)**, **no madvise round trips (Mesa 0002)**, **wrapped surfaces not
   hashed (userland 0015)**: smaller, measured gains each.
6. **Hitches: closing a buffer waited for the GPU (x57).** The kernel's `freeBuffer()` waited up to 2 s for the
   buffer's last job, with the driver's lock held. `glslow=4000` (x56) showed `glDeleteTextures` over 4 ms 191 times
   in about 35 s of page swipes, 7 ms each. A buffer whose job has not ended is now marked and freed at a later
   request: once in x57. What is left over 4 ms is CoreAnimation's own `glFinishObjectAPPLE` (63 times), which
   has to wait for the whole queue because the jobs run in order.

Tried and not kept: the GPU job thread on an A55 (x34: no change, kept as it is harmless); drawing straight into the
surface (`glblit=0`, x47: fragment jobs 600 to 700 us, more late frames); relaxing panfrost's 64-byte row rule (not
tried: research says it is a hardware limit on this GPU generation, an 8-bit image with 480-byte rows faulted on a G52);
running tiler and fragment jobs side by side (not possible: Mesa gives all batches one tiler heap); no preload under
the frame's copy into its surface (Mesa 0006, x54: nothing gained within what differs from run to run, reverted).

Runs of the same build differ by about six points of that percentage, and the log holds only a session's last 20 s
or so, so a change of a few percent cannot be told from one run.

## The window server starts anew now and then (open)

In three of about thirty sessions nobody watched (x46, x52, x61) backboardd and SpringBoard came up a second time in
the middle of the session: new process numbers, the frame count from nothing. The log ring then holds only their
coming up, so why the first ones went is not known. It is not the GPU job that failed in every session's log: that
was the first job of each boot (next section). To catch the next one, kernel patch 0016 keeps a fatal exception or
an abort of those two programs (with up to eight callers) in the notes, which are then said with every 5 s report of
the GPU service, and the bridge's summary says where it and libOSMesa are loaded ("images: this bridge at ...").
`C:\begonia-private\sh\auto_check.sh TAG` shows both. x62 to x74 and x76 to x90 ran without one.

x75 was a fourth odd session of another kind: the phone said it was unlocked, backboardd had taken the touch screen,
all 2,052 touches of the script were sent, and nothing on the screen moved for the whole session (the idle 10 frames
a second, 344 frames). So SpringBoard, or what brings touches to it, stood still. If iOS's own watchdog ends such a
SpringBoard, that would be the starting anew of the other three, with no exception to keep. The sessions from x78
on ran with `ustacks=SpringBoard ustacks_at=45 ustacks_ms=8000 ustacks_threads=1`, which prints SpringBoard's main
thread every 8 s ("ustacks:" lines); in the sessions that went well it waits in its run loop (the first address
ends in ff0, `mach_msg2_trap`). A session that stands still will show where it waits instead.

Over the scripted sessions of the final code (x57 to x90, without the four above): home-screen page swipes 75 to
90% of intervals under 18 ms and none or 1% over 35; Settings scrolling 73 to 92%; the emoji keyboard 64 to 68%,
with 7 to 14% over 35 ms (the GPU's limit).

## The first GPU job of every boot failed (fixed, x65)

`init_gpu_hardware()` writes the address space's memory attributes and configuration only when there are page
tables, and at start-up there are none; `flush_tables()` later wrote the table's address alone. The first job then
could not read its own chain (JS_STATUS 0x42 after 400 ms), the kernel reset the GPU, and only that recovery set the
address space up. `flush_tables()` writes all of it now. This was the "FAILED (1)" in every session's notes.

## Tearing

The display driver already knew how to write each share of the rows behind the panel's scan (`fb_beam`); it lacked a
line counter for this SoC. Kernel 0013 gives it DISP_RDMA0's (`fb_line=0x1400b12c`), which is now the default, 20 s
after the driver starts (`fb_beam_wait=`). The driver counts shares written across the scan: none in x46, x48, x49.
With it on, intervals spread a little (x46: 76% under 18 ms, 23% between 18 and 26, none over 35): every frame still
has a refresh of its own. `fb_line=0` puts frames back on their beat.

The window server is still paced by a timer of 1/60 s (16.667 ms) while the panel's scan takes 16.603 ms, so the
two slide past each other every few seconds; the same build and gestures gave 91% of intervals under 18 ms in one
session and 73% in another (x63, x66). Kernel 0017, `fb_vsync_scan=1`, times each refresh told to the window server
from the line counter instead. One light session with it had 92% (x67, Settings), one heavy one 75% (x68, home
pages): not shown to be better, so it is off unless asked for.

## How it was found

- **Per-call timing in the bridge** (userland 0017, 0018, 0021): time inside GL calls against CoreAnimation's own, by
  call, and Mesa's own draw against the bridge around it. CoreAnimation was never the cost (1 to 2 ms a frame).
- **Flat sampling profile** (userland 0020, `glprof=2000`): by 64-byte piece of each image and by image. The thirty
  stacks printed before were a third of the samples. It showed 41% of the drawing thread in kernel calls, and
  `__psynch_mutexwait` (first misread as `mach_vm_deallocate`: `dladdr` names the nearest exported symbol).
  `C:\begonia-private\sh\sym.sh`, `prof.py` and `flat.py` symbolize against the unstripped libOSMesa.
- **Kernel counters** (`mcp=1`): cache cleaning by stage and every GPU request by kind, count and time
  (`mali-cache:`, `mali-calls`), GPU jobs by kind and by length (`BEGONIA GPU JOBS`, `BEGONIA GPU SIZES`).
- **The frame-interval histogram** of the display driver ("from one frame to the next") is the measure to use.
  Frames a second over five seconds is not: it counts the pauses between gestures.
- **Screenshots in the log** (kernel 0015): the boxes were seen, counted and seen gone without anybody at the phone.

## Open

- **GPU time in heavy scenes.** About 21 fragment jobs a frame on the home screen, 13 to 14 ms; four or five of them
  are full-screen passes of 1 to 4 ms (CoreAnimation coming back to the picture around each blur, and the frame's copy
  into its surface), and they are 80% of the GPU's time. The memory is at its top rate (4264 Mbps computed from the PHY's
  registers, shuffle level 0, x49) and the GPU at its top stock clock, so this is work to be saved, not speed to be found.
- **The window server starting anew** in about one session in ten (see above), cause unknown.
- **Pacing against the panel** (`fb_vsync_scan=1`): needs more sessions than two to judge.
- **mcs=1** trusts the IOSurface seed for cache cleaning. Nothing stale was seen in any screenshot; `mcs=0` if it is.
